What FileBot is

FileBot describes itself as the ultimate tool for renaming and organizing movies, TV shows and anime. It matches files against TheMovieDB, TheTVDB or AniDB, organizes them into a standard structure for Plex, Kodi and Jellyfin, and can fetch subtitles and artwork. It also renames music by ID3 tags or AcoustID fingerprints and photos by Exif data, and it has a command line and scripting engine for automated setups. It runs on Windows, Mac and Linux, with packages for Synology, QNAP, unRAID and Docker.

Where Media Renamer Pro is different

  • Your whole library in one window. Movies, TV, photos, music, audiobooks and game ROMs each get their own lookups and naming presets. Audiobooks and ROMs are not on FileBot's feature list.
  • It runs on your iPhone and iPad too. One purchase covers Mac, iPhone and iPad, including files on a NAS you can reach through the Files app.
  • It shows its work. Every row gets a confidence percentage based on real evidence, and hovering shows why. When the tags inside a file disagree with its name, you pick the winner before anything is renamed.
  • It fixes the file, not just the name. Version 2.0 edits title, artist, album, show, year and cover art inside MP3, MP4 and MKV files.
  • Nothing to set up. No account, no license key, no telemetry. Tokens are click-to-add, with no regex or scripting needed.

Price

Media Renamer Pro is US$4.99, once, for Mac, iPhone and iPad. On November 2, 2026, filebot.net listed a one-year license at US$6 and a lifetime license at US$48. FileBot's Mac App Store version is US$79.99, and FileBot notes that store purchases do not include its universal license.
