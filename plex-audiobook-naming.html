Plex audiobook naming: how to set up audiobooks in Plex so they sort and resume

Plex has no audiobook library. It treats a book like an album of music, so the names and tags have to follow the music rules. Get those right and Plex groups each book under its author, plays the parts in order and remembers where you stopped.

By Buck Kettering· November 22, 2026· 5 min read

Short answer. Put audiobooks in their own Music library and turn on Store track progress for it. Treat the author as the album artist and the book as the album: Audiobooks/Sarah J. Maas/Crown of Midnight (2013)/01 - Crown of Midnight.mp3. Give every part a track number tag, and make the folder names and the tags say the same thing.

Plex was built for movies, TV and music. Audiobooks ride along in a Music library, which means Plex reads them the way it reads albums. Most audiobook trouble in Plex (books split in two, parts out of order, a book that never shows up) is a music rule being broken somewhere.

1. Make a separate Music library

Create a new library, pick the Music type and name it Audiobooks. Point it only at your audiobook folder, never at the same folder as your music.

Then open the library's settings, go to Advanced and turn on Store track progress. That is the setting that lets Plex and Plexamp resume a book where you stopped. You want it here and not on your music library, where resuming a song halfway through is just annoying. That is the main reason to keep the two libraries apart.

2. Author is the artist, the book is the album

Plex groups music by the tags inside the files, and the album artist tag matters most. For a book, map the fields like this:

  • Album artist: the author. Use one spelling for every book by that author, or Plex makes two author pages.
  • Album: the book title.
  • Track number: the part number. Add a disc number too if the book came on several discs.
  • Title: the part or chapter name. The book title works fine here too.

Turn on Prefer local metadata in the library settings, so Plex trusts your tags instead of trying to match a book to a music album online. If your folder names and your tags disagree, fix the tags first. They are what Plex groups by.

3. One folder per book

Never drop a pile of books into one flat folder. Give each author a folder and each book a folder inside it:

Audiobooks/
  Crown of Midnight Part 1.mp3
  Crown of Midnight Part 2.mp3
  Heir of Fire Part 1.mp3
Audiobooks/
  Sarah J. Maas/
    Crown of Midnight (2013)/
      01 - Crown of Midnight.mp3
      02 - Crown of Midnight.mp3
      cover.jpg

A cover.jpg in the book folder becomes the album art. One more trap: a book or author name made of only a number can be skipped, because Plex tries to match music names against online music data. If a title is just a number, the tags have to carry the full name.

4. Number every part

Plex plays an album in track number order, so a book with no track numbers plays in whatever order Plex finds the files. Set the track number tag on every part, and zero-pad the number at the start of the file name too (01, 02 ... 10) so file managers and other players agree. Without the padding, Part 10 can sort before Part 2.

Single file or many? Store track progress remembers your place inside a track, not across a whole book. A book in one file (an M4B, say) resumes exactly. A book split into 40 parts resumes inside the last part you played, so you need to start from that part.

5. Series have no field of their own

Music has no series field, so Plex has nowhere to put "Book 2 of Throne of Glass". Two common fixes:

  • Put it in the album title: Throne of Glass 2 - Crown of Midnight. The series sorts together on the author page.
  • Leave it out and sort by year. The (2013) in the folder name and the year tag keep the books in reading order for most series.

Pick one and use it for the whole library. Mixing the two is what makes an author page look random.

6. Using Plex and Audiobookshelf together

Plenty of people point both servers at the same files. Audiobookshelf wants an extra series folder (Author/Series/Vol 2 - 2013 - Title {Narrator}/), and it reads the folder names closely. Plex leans on the tags. So one tree can serve both: keep the folders the way Audiobookshelf likes them, and make sure the album artist and album tags say author and book. The full Audiobookshelf layout is in the Audiobookshelf naming convention guide.

7. Third-party agents

Agents like Audnexus pull book details from Audible into Plex. They run on Plex's older plugin agent system, and Plex has been moving away from that system, so treat them as a bonus, not the foundation. Good tags and a clean folder per book work with or without an agent, and they keep working if the agent stops.
